## Proposed paper (one merged CVPR submission)
**"Whoever zooms decides: evidence selection is an attack surface, and evidence debate closes it."**
- **Part A, the threat.** Any model that answers from *selected* evidence (MIL top-K, agent zoom, VLM crops) can be pushed to any answer by
  the selector. This holds on gigapixel pathology and on natural high-resolution VQA.
- **Part B, the defense.** Two selectors argue for opposite answers by revealing evidence, and a question-blind judge decides. This is
  sound when evidence is sparse, and that is exactly where selection is needed.

## What I am confident claiming
1. **The selector is a real attack surface.** Every selected-evidence judge collapses to ≈0 on C16 and BCNB. On V*, one adversarial
   crop flips 64% of answers even with the global view present, and 27% of questions flip under *natural* (non-adversarial) zooms alone.
2. **Debate is sound and preserves accuracy on sparse evidence.** On C16 it holds .94–.97 against greedy and look-ahead liars. Judges retrained
   from scratch agree (no judge hacking), and it beats the random-mask baseline from the LessWrong critique by 25–40 points.
3. **It transfers to natural-image VLMs** with no training (V*).

## Honest negatives (put them in the paper rather than hide them)
- **On diffuse labels (BCNB), debate ≈ the random-mask ensemble.** This is the LessWrong "debate ≈ random masking" critique and it holds there.
  Framing: debate's advantage scales with evidence sparsity. C16 vs BCNB is the controlled contrast, and it should be shown as a figure.
- **The honest side must be competent.** A random honest debater loses (.15–.46). This is the expected soundness condition; state it as an assumption.
- **The prediction that "hiding is easy, faking is hard" is false on C16.** Attacks succeed in both directions.
- **Faithfulness:** debate's revealed patches hit lesions only slightly more often than attention top-K. Claim soundness, not better explanations.
- **Lying consultant on BCNB = .24–.39.** That is not 0, so a single liar has a harder time with diffuse labels. It is still far below debate.

## Update Oct 10: evidence-sparsity sweep (CAMELYON16, 3 seeds; full table in `narval_results/sparsity_table.txt`)
**Intervention:** tumor slides keep only t tumor patches (cap1/4/16/64); the other tumor patches and their 8-neighbours (borderline tumor) are dropped.
Train and test are treated the same way. "dens" = normal tissue is subsampled instead (minimum bag size 64).

**Key fix:** if the debate judge is trained on random patches first ("cold"), it never sees a tumor patch when tumor is very rare, so debate fails (cap1 K4 .67).
Warm-starting the judge on attention-selected patches ("warm") fixes this. Test-time debate is unchanged.

| K=4 balacc | cap1 | cap4 | cap16 | cap64 | natural | dens 5% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| debate (warm judge) | .92 | .96 | .97 | .93 | .96 | .97 |
| debate vs look-ahead liar | .92 | .95 | .97 | .92 | .96 | .97 |
| fresh judge on the transcripts | .94 | .96 | .96 | .98 | .97 | .97 |
| random-mask ensemble (32) | .51 | .49 | .52 | .52 | .63 | .65 (.90 at K16) |
| attention top-K, honest | .93 | .98 | .99 | .97 | .98 | .97 |
| attention top-K, attacked | .00 | .00 | .00 | .00 | .00 | .00 |
| V-IP querier | .51 | .57 | .51 | .59 | .79 | .87 |

**Story:** among selected-evidence methods, debate is the only one that is both accurate and robust at every sparsity level. The selector-free baseline
(random masks) is robust but only accurate when evidence is dense. Cooperative selectors are accurate but are driven to 0 by a dishonest selector.
This is the central figure.

**Running now:** dens0.05/0.2 rerun with min bag 64; Qwen3-VL-8B on V*; Qwen2.5-VL on HR-Bench-4K (200 questions); and the DeepEyes-7B crop-substitution attack (191 questions).
A 3-question DeepEyes smoke test was easy: none of the adversarial crops flipped it. The full run will show whether an agent that keeps the full-resolution image in context is robust.
