### A — novelty verdict

- **Pure MIL version (adversarial selector drives a top-K MIL judge to 3–5% BA)**: weak on its own. Reviewers will say "of course top-K over adversarially chosen instances can be steered". BagShift already makes the non-adversarial "selector matters" point on CAMELYON16 and PANDA. Not CVPR-main novelty alone.
- **Agent version (natural selectors — LLM planners such as GIANT, question-similarity retrieval, BEACON-style information gain, saliency — steer answers on the same slide)**: this is still open, and it is the paper. The "selector sensitivity" metric needs to be formal (for example, the spread of answers or accuracy over a selector family at a fixed patch budget, plus a "steerability" bound: the best achievable flip rate within the selector family). Also needed:
  1. Several real agents (GIANT, CPathAgent/SlideSeek-style, PathFinder) **and** natural-image zoom agents (DeepEyes, Mini-o3, ZoomEye, Chain-of-Focus) on V*/HR-Bench. Cross-domain generality is what makes it a CVPR paper rather than a MICCAI one.
  2. A taxonomy: adversarial < biased (e.g., hypothesis-conditioned) < natural selectors. Show that natural selectors reach a meaningful fraction of the adversarial flip rate.
  3. Mechanism: *why* (confirmation-bias loops, where the selector is conditioned on the agent's current hypothesis).
  4. A fix or diagnostic (e.g., selector-ensembling, counter-evidence quota, or Paper B as the remedy). An audit-only paper is borderline for CVPR main.

### B — novelty verdict

- The protocol itself is a decade-old idea (Irving 2018, Merlin-Arthur, PVG). The new parts are the setting (gigapixel MIL, lesion-annotated ground truth, a question-blind judge) and the claims (image-necessity and faithfulness measured against lesion masks). For CVPR main, "debate on CAMELYON16" alone risks being called an application paper.
- To be CVPR-competitive:
  1. **Beat the random-mask / judge-ensemble baseline clearly.** The LessWrong result says debate barely beats it on natural images. Also beat single-prover (consultancy / Merlin-only), V-IP-style cooperative selection, and attention top-K. Matched patch budget K is mandatory.
  2. **Soundness under a strong, optimized liar.** Use RL- or gradient-trained adversarial debaters (reuse Paper A's adversarial selector as the liar). Report the liar win-rate vs K and the soundness/completeness of the Merlin-Arthur metrics.
  3. **Faithfulness with ground truth.** Lesion hit-rate on CAMELYON16 vs FOCI, GCE-MIL, attention, and BagShift's tumor-retention numbers.
  4. **Generality beyond one dataset.** CAMELYON16 plus at least one subtyping or grading task (PANDA, BRACS, BCNB) plus a natural high-res set (V*/HR-Bench), with LLM agents as debaters.
  5. Ideally **one paper, not two**: A (selectors steer answers, adversarial ≈ liar) motivates B (debate is a selector-robust protocol). Merged, this looks like a CVPR main paper. Separately, A alone is a solid audit (possibly CVPR or NeurIPS D&B) and B alone is a MICCAI-level application unless the soundness results are strong.

## Honest bottom line

- **A**: The novel bit is "natural agent selectors steer answers, and steerability is measurable", across WSI and natural-image zoom agents. The MIL adversarial result is supporting evidence, not the contribution. Cite and differentiate **BagShift** up front: it covers non-adversarial selectors for MIL only, with no agents, no question-conditioning, and no adversarial bound.
- **B**: Novel in setting, not in mechanism. It must show a clear win over random-mask and single-prover baselines and soundness against an optimized liar. Otherwise it reads as Irving 2018 / Merlin-Arthur applied to CAMELYON16.
- **Strongest framing**: merge them. The selector is the attack surface (A), and adversarial two-sided selection is the defense with guarantees (B).
